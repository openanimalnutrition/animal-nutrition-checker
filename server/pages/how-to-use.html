How to use the Open Animal Nutrition nutrient checker

Last revised 28 October 2026, for checker release 0.10.1.

The checker compares a dog or cat diet's nutrient values with one requirement (NRC, AAFCO or FEDIAF). It tells you where each value lies between that requirement's minimum and range high, in the Dynamic Decile System's band names, with a proposed verdict.

The checker's web page is not an AI. It does not read labels or reports, and it does not convert units. It takes numbers, each already per 1,000 kcal ME (per Mcal). If you have a label, a recipe report or an AI's nutrient profile as it is, use way 3: an AI assistant connected to the checker reads and converts it, and the checker does the comparison.

Converting to per Mcal

Every value goes in per Mcal (1,000 kcal ME), in the unit shown on its row. Use the same basis (as fed, or dry matter) for both numbers in each formula.

  • From a percentage: g/Mcal = % × 10,000 ÷ kcal ME per kg. 26% protein in a food with 3,800 kcal ME/kg: 26 × 10,000 ÷ 3,800 = 68.4 g/Mcal.
  • From an amount per kg: amount per Mcal = amount per kg ÷ (kcal ME per kg ÷ 1,000).
  • Per 1,000 kcal is already per Mcal. Per 100 kcal: multiply by 10.
  • The Energy Density row is kcal per gram of dry matter: 3,800 kcal/kg as fed at 10% moisture is 3.8 ÷ 0.90 = 4.22 kcal/g DM.

Way 1: type the values

Example: a 20 kg adult dog, against AAFCO Dog Maintenance, eating a dry food whose label gives crude protein 26%, crude fat 16%, calcium 1.2%, phosphorus 1.0%, moisture 10% and 3,800 kcal ME/kg. Per Mcal: protein 68.4, fat 42.1, calcium 3.16, phosphorus 2.63 (g/Mcal); energy density 4.22 kcal/g DM.

To see it without typing, open the worked example, already filled in, and click Check. To type it yourself:

  1. Open the checker's first page.
  2. Under Choose a requirement, choose AAFCO Dog Maintenance (in the group "Dogs: AAFCO"). Click Next.
  3. Under Animal and energy, type 20 in Current body weight (kg).
  4. In Type of adult dog, choose the one that fits, for example Average laboratory or active (130 * kg BW^0.75). The other boxes are optional.
  5. Leave What is entered as diet.
  6. In the table, type 68.4 in the Low (Min entry) box of Protein. Leave High (Max entry) empty.
  7. Do the same for Fat, Total Lipid (42.1), Calcium, Ca (3.16), Phosphorus, P (2.63) and, under Energy, Energy Density (4.22).
  8. Click Check.

Low and High are the diet's own values, not the standard's limits. The checker already has the limits. Most diets have one value per nutrient: type it in Low. Use both boxes only for a range, such as an analysed minimum and maximum. Leave every other row empty.

Reading the result

The result opens with what it is not (not a safety or compliance assessment, not veterinary advice), then the animal and energy used, an input report, and one block per nutrient. For this example:

  • Band: where the value lies, from "Very Severely Restricted (<25% of Min)" to "Highest (90% of Range or Greater)".
  • Verdict: deficient, excess or none (proposed, pending ratification).
  • Range high: each says what kind of maximum it is. Protein's 180 is the minimum × 4, a banding choice, not a safe limit.
  • Input report: any value that was not used, and why. For this example it says "Every value entered was used; nothing is missing."
  • Only the nutrients entered are checked. This example checks six; it says nothing about the rest of the diet.

Change the inputs takes you back with everything kept. Download JSON saves the result.

Way 2: paste the values

Do steps 1 to 5 of way 1, then:

  1. In Or paste values instead of typing them, put one nutrient per line: the name, a comma, then the value. For the example:
    • Protein, 68.4
    • Fat, 42.1
    • Calcium, 3.16
    • Phosphorus, 2.63
    • Energy Density, 4.22
  2. Click Parse the pasted lines. The page shows how many rows it read, lists any line it did not understand, and fills the table.
  3. Look over the table, then click Check.

The paste box reads only this format:

  • Numbers only: no units, % signs or colons, and each value already per Mcal.
  • One value: "Protein, 68.4". A range: "Protein, 60, 70". Only a high: "Protein, , 70".
  • The name must be the row's name as the table shows it, or its id (below). Other names are not understood: not "Ca", "Vitamin A", "Linoleic acid" or "Crude protein".
  • From a spreadsheet: copy the name, low and high columns; tabs work as well as commas.

"Not understood" means the name did not match a row, the line had no comma or tab, or the nutrient appeared twice.

Way 3: an AI assistant

Connect the checker to an AI assistant once. Then paste a label, a recipe report or a nutrient profile into the chat as it is. The assistant reads it, converts the units, asks for the animal's facts, sends the values to the checker and explains the result. The checker does the comparison; the assistant can still misread the source, so check the values it sent.

The checker's address for AI assistants, no sign-in:

What to ask

Give the animal's facts and the requirement up front. Otherwise assistants tend to assume them. For example:

Use the Open Animal Nutrition checker. Compare this diet with AAFCO Dog Maintenance for a 20 kg adult spayed pet dog, walked twice a day. Afterwards, list each value you entered, per Mcal, and how you converted it.

[paste the label, recipe report or nutrient profile here]

  • Check the values it entered against your source. A slip (for example, 10 times too high) often shows up as an implausible band or an excess, but not always.
  • The checker's words are the result: its band names and verdicts. "Meets AAFCO" or "complete and balanced" in a summary is the assistant's wording, not the checker's.
  • Privacy: the checker keeps no record of what is entered. What you type into an assistant is covered by its provider's terms. A temporary or incognito chat keeps the diet out of your chat history and memory.

If something goes wrong

  • Every pasted line is "Not understood": the text has units, colons or other names. Retype it as in way 2, or paste it into an AI assistant (way 3).
  • A value is "Not used": the input report says why (for example, text instead of a number).
  • An error, or a result you think is wrong: say how on the About page (public issues, or email).
